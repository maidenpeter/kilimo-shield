Questions

Short answers. Each links to the full explanation.

Insurers and reinsurers

{% call q("Who carries the risk?") %}

The partner insurer (to be named), and any reinsurer behind it. AgroShield builds and runs the index, the pricing and the monitoring, and never pays a claim on its own: every payout waits for the underwriter's approval.

{% endcall %} {% call q("Can we reproduce a premium?") %}

Yes. Every pricing run saves its inputs, config hashes and a results hash. Re-running with the same inputs gives the same hash. How pricing works.

{% endcall %} {% call q("Which rainfall data do you use?") %}

CHIRPS, a satellite and rain gauge record from the Climate Hazards Center at UC Santa Barbara that starts in 1981{{ ui.cite(5) }}. Pricing uses 1981 to 2025.

{% endcall %} {% call q("Is there a legal deadline for payouts?") %}

Kenya's draft index insurance regulations were reported to require payouts within ten days of the trigger{{ ui.cite(3) }}. They were still a draft in February 2026{{ ui.cite(4) }}. More on the draft.

{% endcall %} {% call q("What is not ready yet?") %}

The partner insurer, the pilot SACCO and wards, loadings, subsidy terms and the review of the consent wording. The full list is on For insurers.

{% endcall %}

SACCOs and farmers

{% call q("How much does it cost?") %}

It depends on the ward, the acres and the cover chosen. A SACCO officer can show a quote in a few minutes, including any subsidy and discount. All prices are illustrative until the product is filed.

{% endcall %} {% call q("Does the government pay part of it?") %}

The State Department for Agriculture describes a 50% premium subsidy for farmers insuring 0.5 to 20 acres{{ ui.cite(2) }}. AgroShield shows that share as "not yet received" until it is paid. About the subsidy.

{% endcall %} {% call q("Why can I not buy once the rains start?") %}

Sales of index cover must close before cover begins, so nobody can wait to see the weather first{{ ui.cite(1) }}. That keeps the price fair for everyone.

{% endcall %} {% call q("My crop failed but there was no payout. What now?") %}

That is called basis risk: the index follows the ward, not each field. Report it by USSD or through your SACCO. Every report is recorded and reviewed to improve the cover, but it does not change a payout. Basis risk, explained.

{% endcall %} {% call q("Can the payout go to my SACCO loan?") %}

Yes, if you agree when you enrol. Otherwise it goes to you by M-Pesa.

{% endcall %} {% call q("Is there cover for livestock?") %}

Not yet. It is in development. Register interest.
