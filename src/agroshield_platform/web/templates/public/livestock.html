The idea

Satellites measure how green the land is. The usual measure, NDVI, runs from zero for no vegetation to close to one for the densest green leaves{{ ui.cite(1) }}. A pasture index built from it can show when grazing in an area has failed, without anyone counting animals.

Kenya has done this before. The IBLI pilot began in Marsabit in January 2010, using satellite vegetation data to predict livestock deaths{{ ui.cite(2) }}. The government's KLIP programme later redesigned its cover to pay at the start of a drought, so herders could buy fodder, water and veterinary care while animals were still alive{{ ui.cite(3) }}.

What we are checking first

Before anything is offered, the data has to be good enough. This work is experimental.

{{ ui.icon('map') }}

Is the data there?

Which free satellite vegetation records reach pastoral areas such as Marsabit, Isiolo and Samburu, and how many years they go back.

{{ ui.icon('pulse') }}

Would it have paid?

How often a pasture trigger set at roughly one bad season in five would have fired in past years, and whether those years match known droughts.

{{ ui.icon('shield') }}

Who would carry it?

Cover would be underwritten by a partner insurer (to be named), like the crop cover. Nothing is agreed yet.
