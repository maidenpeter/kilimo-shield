One failed season shouldn't undo a lifetime of work.

AgroShield protects the season before it fails, and rewards farmers who farm smarter. Maize cover for SACCO members, priced on your ward's own rainfall history and paid on a measured index, not a farm visit.

How it works SACCO officer sign in

What is this cover?

A simple promise: you pay a small premium each season through your SACCO. If the rains in your ward fail badly enough, measured the same way for everyone, you receive a payout so you can recover, replant and keep going.

It protects your season

Failed rains are the biggest threat to a maize crop. Cover turns an unpredictable loss into a known cost you can plan for.

It can help before you lose

If the rains fail soon after you plant, cover pays for replanting seed as a voucher, so the season can still be saved.

It keeps families on the land

A single drought can force a family to sell assets or take on debt. A timely payout, or a payment straight to your SACCO loan if you choose, breaks that cycle.

How it works

No paperwork after a loss and no assessor at your gate. Payouts follow rainfall data for your ward that anyone can check.

Your season is measured

Satellite rainfall for your ward is tracked from the first planting rain to harvest, together with how much water a maize crop needs.

The index is worked out

The index compares the water the crop got with the water it needed. The drier the season, the lower the score.

The payout is calculated

Below the agreed level, the payout grows as the season gets worse, up to your full sum insured if the crop would have failed.

Money reaches you

You get an SMS and the payout goes to you or, if you agreed, first to your SACCO loan. The system is built to pay within 10 days of the index confirming.

Honest about the trade-off. The cover pays on your ward's index, not a visit to your field. That keeps premiums low and payouts quick, but a very local loss can fall outside the index. If your farm failed and no payout came, tell your SACCO: every case is recorded and reviewed to improve the cover, although it does not change a payout.

Why SACCO members choose it

No assessor, no disputes

The same public rainfall data decides every payout, so the rules are clear and fair.

Often subsidised

The government's KAIP programme can pay part of the premium for eligible farms of 0.5 to 20 acres.

Rewards good farming

Certified seed and good practices earn Resilience points that lower your share of next season's premium.

Any phone works

Enrol with your SACCO officer and check your cover by USSD on any phone, in English or Kiswahili.

Priced on your ward

Premiums come from your ward's own rainfall history since 1981, not a national average.

Paid through your SACCO

Pay by SACCO deduction or M-Pesa, and link payouts to your loan if you wish.

For SACCOs, insurers and reinsurers

The pricing behind every quote is reproducible: rainfall inputs, index rules and loadings are versioned and every pricing run is logged, so an underwriter can rebuild any premium from the raw data.

Questions farmers ask

How much does it cost?

It depends on your ward, acreage and the cover you choose. Your SACCO officer can show you a quote in a few minutes, including any government subsidy and your Resilience discount.

What if my farm fails but my ward's index does not pay?

That is called basis risk. Because payouts follow the ward index, a very local loss can fall outside it. Report it to your SACCO: it is recorded and reviewed to improve the cover, but it does not change the payout.

How quickly do I get paid?

The system is built to calculate and pay within 10 days of the index confirming a payout. You are told by SMS.

Do I need a smartphone or a bank account?

No. You enrol through your SACCO and can check your cover by USSD on any phone. Payouts go to M-Pesa, your SACCO account or your SACCO loan.

What happens if the rains fail right after I plant?

If too little rain falls in the weeks after you plant, the cover gives you a voucher for replanting seed, once a season, and the season is measured again from your next planting.
