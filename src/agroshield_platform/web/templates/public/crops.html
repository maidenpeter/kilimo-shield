What the cover pays for

All amounts and levels below are illustrative until the partner insurer (to be named) files the product.

{{ ui.icon('sun') }}

A dry season

The crop water index (WRSI) scores how well the season's rain met the maize crop's needs, where 100 means no shortfall{{ ui.cite(1) }}. Nothing is paid at 80 or above. Below 80 the payout grows, up to the full sum insured at 50 or below, the level FEWS NET treats as crop failure{{ ui.cite(1) }}. All payouts in one season together never exceed the sum insured, and a seed voucher counts toward it.

{{ ui.icon('rain') }}

Rains that never start

If less than 80 mm of rain falls over the whole planting window, the season is treated as never started and a quarter of the sum insured is paid.

{{ ui.icon('seed') }}

Rain that stops after planting

If less than 20 mm falls in the 20 days after the planting rain, the farmer gets a replanting seed voucher (KES 2,500 per acre, illustrative), at most once a season. The season is then measured again from the next planting rain.

How a farmer buys it

  1. 1

    Enrol with the SACCO

    The SACCO officer records the farm, the acres and the cover chosen, and asks for consent in English or Kiswahili.

  2. 2

    Pay before sales close

    Premium can be paid in instalments by SACCO deduction or M-Pesa. Sales close well before planting, so nobody can buy after seeing the weather{{ ui.cite(4) }}. Cover starts only once the premium is fully paid.

  3. 3

    The ward is watched all season

    Satellite rainfall for the ward{{ ui.cite(2) }} is checked every day. The farmer gets an SMS when a payout or a seed voucher is due.

  4. 4

    Payout, if the index says so

    The insurer approves the payout and it goes to the farmer by M-Pesa or, if the farmer agreed, first to their SACCO loan.

Who pays the premium

Kenya's State Department for Agriculture describes a 50% premium subsidy for farmers insuring 0.5 to 20 acres of the insured crop{{ ui.cite(3) }}. Whether this cover qualifies is not confirmed yet, so quotes treat that share as an assumption. AgroShield shows it separately and marks it "not yet received" until the money arrives. Outside 0.5 to 20 acres, no subsidy is assumed.

Farmers who used certified seed or good practices last season can earn a discount on their own share. The insurer always receives the full premium: the discount is paid by a named funder, and it does not change how payouts work.

About the subsidy

The honest trade-off

The cover pays on the ward's index, not on a visit to each field. That keeps it cheap to run and quick to pay, but a very local loss can fall outside the index. This is called basis risk.

If a farmer's crop failed and no payout came, or it was too small, they can report it by USSD or through their SACCO. Every report is recorded and reviewed to improve the index. A report does not change a payout.
