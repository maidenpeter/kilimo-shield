How it works

From rainfall to payout, step by step

AgroShield is the technology, pricing and monitoring layer. Cover is underwritten by a partner insurer (to be named) and sold through SACCOs.

{{ ui.hero_credit('chasing-rain-mara') }}

Illustrative terms. The pricing and the payout levels on this page are illustrative, and the pilot wards are placeholders, until the partner insurer files the product. They are the terms the system runs today.

1. Measure the season

The crop cover uses a seasonal Water Requirement Satisfaction Index (WRSI) for each ward. WRSI compares the water a crop actually used with the water it needed, and a score of 100 means no shortfall{{ ui.cite(2) }}.

  • Rainfall: CHIRPS satellite rainfall for each ward, 1981 to 2025{{ ui.cite(1) }}.
  • Water balance: a ten-day (dekad) water balance using maize crop water needs at each growth stage.
  • Start of season: the first dekad with at least 25 mm of rain in the ward's planting window.
  • Triggers are set per ward, never per county.

Crop water need also depends on evaporation. Until the FEWS NET evaporation record can be downloaded, the engine estimates it from NASA POWER temperatures (FAO-56 Hargreaves), and every output says so.

{{ ui.picture('maize-siaya', 'r-4x3') }}

2. Decide the payout

  • Drought payout: nothing at WRSI 80 or above, rising in a straight line to the full sum insured at WRSI 50 or below. FEWS NET treats a seasonal WRSI below 50 as crop failure{{ ui.cite(2) }}.
  • Failed onset: if less than 80 mm falls over the whole planting window, the season never started and 25% of the sum insured is paid.
  • Replanting seed voucher: if less than 20 mm falls in the 20 days after the planting rain, a seed voucher worth KES 2,500 per acre is issued, at most once a season, and the season is measured again from the next planting rain.
  • Season cap: all payouts in one season together never exceed the sum insured, and a seed voucher counts toward it.

3. Price it, so anyone can check

The engine runs the same index over every past season from 1981 to 2025 and works out what the cover would have paid each year. The premium is that average payout plus a risk margin, grossed up for costs:

Gross premium (illustrative)

(expected loss + risk margin) / (1 - loadings)

Loadings today: expenses 12%, SACCO distribution 5%, AgroShield platform 6%, underwriter margin 7%. All are placeholders for the insurer to set.

What every run records

The index version, the hash of every config file, checksums of the rainfall and evaporation inputs, the code version and a hash of the results. Re-running with the same inputs gives the same hash.

Sales close before cover starts, so nobody can wait to see the weather before buying{{ ui.cite(3) }}. Government subsidy and the farmer's Resilience discount change who pays, never the price of the risk.

4. Watch every ward, every day

Daily monitoring runs the same index on the latest rainfall, with each ward's usual rainfall standing in for the rest of the season. Nothing is final until the data covers it. Wards close to a trigger are flagged early so the insurer and SACCOs can prepare.

5. Pay, after the insurer approves

  1. 1

    Index confirmed

    Once the rainfall data covers the trigger, payouts are calculated for every insured farmer in the ward.

  2. 2

    Insurer approves or holds

    The underwriter approves each payout, or holds it with a reason. Kenya's draft index insurance regulations would require payouts within ten days of the trigger{{ ui.cite(4) }}.

  3. 3

    Farmer is paid

    Payouts are designed to be paid within 10 days of the index being confirmed, after the insurer approves them. Confirmation can come some weeks after the trigger, because final rainfall data is published later. Seed vouchers are redeemed at an agro-dealer.

6. When the index misses a loss

A farmer whose crop failed but who got no payout, or too small a payout, can report it by USSD or through their SACCO. Each report is reviewed and kept to improve the index, but a report does not change a payout. Every trigger calculation is versioned and logged, so any result can be checked later.
