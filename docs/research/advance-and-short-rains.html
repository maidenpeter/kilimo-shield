1. Early advance on Layer B (Ward A, long rains)

About 35 days after the start of season, the end-of-season WRSI is projected from observed CHIRPS so far, the CHIRPS-GEFS v12 15-day mean forecast and climatology for the rest of the season. Below 95, the farmer gets 20% of the sum insured at once. At the end of the season the advance is deducted from the Layer B payout and never clawed back, so the farmer receives the larger of the two. Only the extra (advance above the final payout, or the whole advance on a false alarm) is priced.

Reading. Short-rains rain here is well forecast: OND skill is clearly above zero, unlike Nakuru's long rains. But maize on these soils fails often. With a 100-day variety the index pays in 39% and 80% of seasons, far above 1 in 5 to 7, and the payouts are large, so gross premium is a large share of sum insured. An 80-day variety brings Mbooni to the edge of the band, but losses stay severe when they happen. Before an OND ward goes further: (1) real crop calendars and varieties from the county, (2) soil data, (3) FEWS NET PET instead of Hargreaves at low elevation, and (4) whether the crop should be sorghum or green grams rather than maize. A forecast-based layer (Layer A-style) may suit these areas better than it suits Nakuru, because the skill is real.

Caveats

  • All product terms are ILLUSTRATIVE. PET is the Hargreaves fallback, flagged; FEWS NET PET and WRSI are still unavailable.
  • The advance projections use leave-one-out climatology for the unobserved part of each season, so the held-out season never informs its own projection. CHIRPS-GEFS hindcasts start in 2000.
  • Short-rains candidates are research cells, not wards, and are not in config/.
