Seasonal forecast skill for Layer A

Can a pre-season forecast pick out bad long-rains seasons at the Nakuru pilot wards well enough to pay a seed voucher before planting? Measured against our CHIRPS ward rainfall, 1981–2016 for the C3S hindcasts and 1998 onward for GHACOF.

Research, not product terms. Ward locations are placeholders; prices use the illustrative loadings and voucher value. Verification is leave-one-out: a season's own rainfall never sets the tercile boundary it is judged by.

(a) Published evidence

As given in the brief (not re-derived here): Walker et al. (2019) find near-zero GloSea skill for the East African long rains (r = 0.07) and much higher skill for the short rains (r = 0.69); GHACOF consensus skill is low except some skill in the below-normal category over Kenya. Our measurements use a different model (ECMWF SEAS5.1 through C3S), a single 1-degree cell and 36 seasons, so a higher long-rains value here is within sampling noise of "low" and should not be read as contradicting it.
