2. Pricing methodology

The price comes from the AgroShield pricing engine (src/agroshield_pricing). It applies today's index terms to every long-rains season from {{ conf.history.start_year }} to {{ conf.history.end_year }} at each ward (a burn-cost analysis), turns the payouts into an expected loss, adds a risk margin and loadings, and prices each sum-insured option in shillings. Every setting is in config/; changing a term is a settings change, not a code change, and every run records the SHA-256 of every settings file and input.

2.1 The index: seasonal WRSI

The Water Requirement Satisfaction Index (WRSI) is the FAO and FEWS NET measure of how much of the water a crop needed it actually got over the season. The engine computes it on a 10-day (dekad) water balance:

  1. Daily CHIRPS v2.0 rainfall and daily potential evapotranspiration (PET) are summed to dekads.
  2. Failed onset: if the whole planting window gets less than {{ lb.failed_onset.window_rain_below_mm }} mm, the season never started and the failed-onset payout applies instead of WRSI.
  3. Start of season: the first dekad in the window with at least {{ lb.planting_rain_mm }} mm. If the {{ lb.germination_dry_spell.days_after_planting }} days after it bring less than {{ lb.germination_dry_spell.rain_below_mm }} mm, the replanting voucher applies and the season restarts at the next planting rain.
  4. For each dekad of the growing period: the crop's water requirement is its crop coefficient (FEWS NET maize curve: {% for v in lb.kc["values"] %}{{ v }}{{ ', ' if not loop.last }}{% endfor %} at {% for v in lb.kc.points_pct %}{{ v }}{{ ', ' if not loop.last }}{% endfor %}% of the season) times PET. A soil bucket holds the ward's water capacity; the crop is stressed once the soil is drier than {{ ((1 - lb.depletion_fraction) * 100)|round|int }}% of capacity (depletion fraction {{ lb.depletion_fraction }}). {{ lb.spinup_dekads }} dekads of bare-soil balance before the start put pre-season rain in the soil.
  5. WRSI = 100 x total water the crop used / total water it needed, at the end of the season.
  6. Payout share: 0 at or above {{ lb.trigger_wrsi }}, rising in a straight line to 100% at or below {{ lb.exit_wrsi }} (crop failure).

Source: src/agroshield_pricing/wrsi.py and README.md. PET is FAO-56 Hargreaves from NASA POWER temperature, shifted to ward elevation ({{ conf.pet.lapse_rate_c_per_km }} C per km), flagged as a FALLBACK: {{ conf.pet.fewsnet_status }}

2.2 Payout per acre in a season

Failed onset: {{ lb.failed_onset.payout|pct }} of sum insured, and nothing else. Otherwise: the WRSI share of sum insured, plus the replanting voucher (a fixed KES amount per acre) if the germination dry spell fired. Capped at the sum insured. Because the voucher is a fixed amount, the engine prices each sum-insured option separately.

2.3 From payouts to premium

{% set pm = conf.premium %}
  • Burn cost. The payout of every season, as a share of sum insured. Expected loss is the mean; the 1-in-10 and 1-in-20 losses are the 90th and 95th percentiles of the seasons.
  • Risk margin. Method {{ pm.risk_margin.method }}: {{ pm.risk_margin.factor }} x the standard deviation of the season losses.
  • Pure premium = expected loss + risk margin.
  • Gross premium = pure premium / (1 - sum of loadings), with loadings as shares of gross: {% for k, v in pm.loadings.items() %}{{ k|replace('_', ' ') }} {{ v|pct }}{{ ', ' if not loop.last }}{% endfor %} (total {{ pm.loadings.values()|sum|pct }}). The intermediary loading is the SACCO's distribution fee; the platform loading is AgroShield's fee.

2.4 Who pays

{% set sb, rs = conf.subsidy, conf.resilience %}

The insurer always receives the full gross premium. The government ({{ sb.programme }}, Kenya Agricultural Insurance Programme) pays {{ sb.government_share|pct }} of gross for farms of {{ sb.eligible_acres.min }} to {{ sb.eligible_acres.max }} acres, recorded as "not yet received" until it arrives (no subsidy outside that band). The member pays the rest, less a Resilience discount of {{ rs.discount_per_point|pct }} per verified good-practice point, capped at {{ rs.discount_cap|pct }} of the member's share, funded by the {{ rs.funder }}, never by the insurer. The discount does not change the risk price: payouts follow the ward index whatever the farmer does. Subsidy terms, the cap and the points are ILLUSTRATIVE pending {{ sb.programme }} and the insurer.

  • Early advance on Layer B: EXPERIMENTAL ({{ lb.advance.status }}), not included in quotes (included_in_quotes: {{ lb.advance.included_in_quotes|lower }}).

6. Basis risk statement

Basis risk is the gap between what the index pays and what a member actually loses. This cover pays on one index per ward, from satellite rainfall averaged over a box {{ conf.wards[0].box_deg }} degrees (about 11 km) wide around a placeholder ward centre. A member's own loss can differ because rain varies within the ward, and because soil, pests, disease, planting date and farming practice differ from farm to farm. Members can lose a crop when the index pays nothing, and the index can pay when a member's crop was fine.

What cannot be measured yet

  • How often the index misses a real crop failure, or pays without one. That needs a history of real maize yields in each insured ward. None is available: {{ A[0].basis_risk.reason }}
  • The real pilot wards. Both wards are placeholders ({% for w in conf.wards %}{{ w.display_name }}{{ ', ' if not loop.last }}{% endfor %}); their locations and soil water capacities are not real ward data.

What is measured or controlled now

  • Agreement with an independent WRSI. The comparison with the FEWS NET WRSI product is built but PENDING: {{ conf.validation.fewsnet_status }}
  • Sensitivity. Payout frequency and price at other trigger levels and soil capacities (sections 3.3 and 3.4).
  • Known event. The 1984 drought is required to pay at least 50% at {{ A[0].ward.display_name }} (section 3.2).
  • Input quality. CHIRPS daily values are spread out from 5-day totals, so the index uses 10-day totals and the flowering dry-spell component was dropped. PET is a flagged fallback (section 2.1).
  • Missed-payout reports. Members report "my crop failed but the payout did not match" by USSD option 5 or through their SACCO officer. Each report records the ward's index status at that moment and is reviewed by a manager with an outcome. Reports never change a payout; they are the evidence for reviewing the index.

The consumer disclosure (section 8) explains basis risk to members in plain words before they buy.

9. Data protection summary

Designed with the Kenya Data Protection Act in mind; the legal review of the consent wording is pending.

  • Encryption at rest. National ID numbers and phone numbers are stored only encrypted (AES-256-GCM, a fresh random nonce per value), with a keyed HMAC-SHA256 for look-ups. Keys come from the environment, never from the code or the database. Officer passwords are stored as scrypt hashes.
  • Masking. IDs and phone numbers are masked on every screen, export, bordereau, audit record and log line (for example +254 7** *** 678). Tests read the raw database file, rendered pages, links and audit rows for any clear ID or phone.
  • Never in a web address. Searches are sent in the request body and enrolment state stays on the server, so personal data never appears in a URL.
  • Local storage. Data stays in the platform's own database (SQLite in development, PostgreSQL in deployment). Public-site enquiries are stored locally and encrypted; nothing is emailed.
  • SMS. Only to members who agreed to SMS. The phone number is decrypted only at the moment of sending and never stored in the outbox. By default (SMS_MODE=log) messages are recorded and not sent.
  • Weather news (KMD and others). Display only: pricing, triggers and payouts never read it (tested). Kenya Meteorological Department forecasts are shown with their label and link after a person approves a summary. No SMS of KMD content until KMD's written permission is on file.
